## Team Audience and Task Research Plan

### Research purpose statement
Choosing to pursue a Master’s degree is a momentous decision that can leave potential students stunned with nescience. This indecision is often worsened by graduate programs that are shrouded in legacies of all-consuming coursework, intense life-school-work balances, and doubtful relativity when transitioning from academia to the job market. For this reason, our group intends to utilize this semester to remove at least one graduate program, Texas State University's Technical Communication Graduate program, from the scrolls of myth and lore and transcribe it to the trusted, accurate FAQ format! This project will confront the question, “What is Technical Communication? And, why would you get a Master’s degree in it?”. To answer this question we will conduct document reviews, expert and non-expert surveys, and utilize personal insight to develop a FAQ that can be transitioned from electronic and paper documents. It is our hope that these documents can be useful for potential students, advisors, and professors in discerning the big-picture question of if obtaining a Master's in Technical Communication with Texas State University is a choice that aligns with an individual’s academic and career goals! 

### Research questions
- What are common misconceptions about the field of technical communication?
- What do people not know about the field of technical communication?
- What might help someone understand the field of technical communication better?

### Method description
This project will center a qualitative approach. The following three methods will be employed: 
- <b>Document Review</b>: Finding and analyzing other relevant documents to determine the standard questions and answers. More specifically, reviewing the current TXST Technical Communication FAQ ([TXST FAQ](https://matc.english.txst.edu/future-students/future-student-faqs.html)) will be necessary to avoid repetition, possibly update information, and ensure that the trajectory of the project is clear and distinct.
- <b>Surveys</b>: Two short surveys sent to our two primary audiences, the experts and the non-experts. The experts can inform us of questions they are normally approached with and their input to what they think is important for interested students to know about the degree/program.  The non-experts will provide insight to common (mis)conceptions, questions, and what information would be useful in their decision to either pursue or forgo Technical Communication as an academic/career path.

### Participant or data source plan
Participants will be majority Texas State University undergraduate students, graduate students, and Technical Communication faculty. 

### Interview, survey, observation, or review protocol
- <b>Document Review Protocol</b>: The group members will compile 4-6 alternative FAQ documents (including the TXST FAQ). Group members will analyze and evaluate the documents to determine differences and similarities, functionality and improvements that they would like to apply to or avoid in their project. 
- <b>Survey Protocol</b>: The group members will develop the individual expert and non-expert surveys according to the agreed upon research questions and the determined project vision identified by the document review. The surveys will be built through [Qualtrics](https://services.txst.edu/TDClient/39/ITAC/Requests/Service/142/Qualtrics). Expert surveys will be sent to all teaching faculty in the Technical Communication program. Non-expert surveys will be sent to the appropriate program staff/coordinator to distribute via the Technical Communication program’s e-mail. Responses to both surveys will be electronically submitted through Qualtrics. 


### Ethics and consent considerations
- <b>Informed Consent</b>: Participants should be given clear information about the purpose of the study. Contact information of the team conducting the study should also be included for participants to use, should there be any questions or concerns related to the study.
- <b>Voluntary Participation</b>: Participants will understand their engagement and participation in the study is voluntary and that they are not obligated to participate.
- <b>Right to Withdraw</b>: Participants will know they are able to withdraw from the study at any time, regardless of how much they have completed the survey.
- <b>Anonymity</b>: Participants will understand their identities will remain hidden.
- <b>Safety</b>: Participants will understand this study will pose minimal to no emotional risk for participants.
- <b>Data Handling</b>: Participants will understand data collected from them will only be used by the team conducting the study and will not be shared with any third parties for related or unrelated purposes.
- <b>AI Usage</b>: Participants will understand AI may be used to clean open-ended data results but will not include any personal data. AI usage (including how AI will be used, when AI will be used, and why AI will be used) will be agreed upon by all members of the team conducting the study.
